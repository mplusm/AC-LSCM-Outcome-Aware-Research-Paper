\section{Re-examining the First Version}
\label{sec:v1}

The first version~\citep{madapathi2026zenodo} reported, on a single ER-10
environment, a safety-violation rate of $0.005 \pm 0.019$ for AC-LSCM over 13
seeds against $0.180 \pm 0.022$ for a Transformer over 3 seeds, and
attributed the gain to the do-operator and to abduction. Its model was the
latent AC-LSCM with a soft do-operator, the NOTEARS acyclicity
penalty~\citep{zheng2018notears} with weight $\lambda_2 = 1$, sparsity weight
$\lambda_1 = 0.1$, adjacency logits initialized at $0$, a soft (relaxed)
adjacency sample during training, a contrastive hinge on the counterfactual
loss, and noise-free counterfactual latents as supervision. We re-ran its
experiments with the released code and found two problems.

\paragraph{The original model learned an empty graph.}
Table~\ref{tab:v1mse} shows that its structural Hamming distance equals the
number of true edges on every graph type, with zero variance across seeds: in
every run, the thresholded adjacency is empty. The first version read
SHD $= 8$ on ER-10 as a precisely learned graph; it is the SHD of the empty
graph. Three causes combine. The logits start exactly at the evaluation
threshold; the sparsity and acyclicity penalties (the latter unnecessary,
since a lagged graph is acyclic by construction) push every logit down; and
with a soft mask, the transition networks can partly compensate for a
shrinking edge, so its gradient never wins against the penalties. With an
empty graph, every factor's prediction depends only on the action. This
explains the first version's high intervention error, its ``DAG constraint
converged exactly to zero'', and its null do-operator ablation, since without
parents there is nothing for the do-operator to sever.

\paragraph{Its agent task tested only direct effects, out of range.}
In that task every candidate action sets either the safety variable or the
goal variable directly, so a model only needs to know that $\doop(z^i = v)$
gives $z^i \approx v$, which a state-blind model knows. Its unsafe values
($1.6$ to $2.5$) also lay outside the training range of action values
($\pm 1.5$). Table~\ref{tab:direct} re-runs it. Under the original protocol,
which evaluated predicates on predicted latents, we reproduce the result. With
predicates evaluated on decoded predictions, the Vanilla VAE and Dreamer-style
baselines also have zero violations; their failures under the original
protocol, like the original AC-LSCM's ``passive'' seeds, came from
thresholding latents that are not aligned with the observed variables, the
problem analysed in Section~\ref{sec:latent}. When the unsafe values are moved
inside the training range, the Transformer's violation rate falls from
$15.6\%$ to $2.0\%$: its failure was a failure to extrapolate action values,
not to represent interventions. The task cannot support the $36\times$ claim,
which we withdraw, together with the claims about precise structure recovery
and about the do-operator. The first version's conclusions that the hinge and
acyclicity terms should be removed are confirmed. Its per-seed results remain
in the repository.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Latent world models and planning.}
Ha and Schmidhuber~\citep{ha2018world} established the template of learning a
compressed latent dynamics model and acting through it; PlaNet~\citep{hafner2019planet}
planned directly in a learned latent space, the Dreamer line~\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}
scaled latent imagination to diverse domains, and MuZero~\citep{schrittwieser2020muzero}
showed that a learned model suffices for strong planning. These models treat
the action as an input to a monolithic transition. Our baselines are small
instances of this design, and our latent AC-LSCM shows a specific risk of
combining it with intervention semantics.

\paragraph{Factored and causal dynamics models.}
Factored representations of transition models have a long history in
planning~\citep{boutilier2000factored}. Causal Dynamics
Learning~\citep{wang2022cdl} learns a sparse causal graph between state
variables and actions to obtain task-independent state abstractions, Ke et
al.~\citep{ke2021systematic} evaluate causal-discovery-based world models in
model-based reinforcement learning, and CoDA~\citep{pitis2020coda} uses
locally factored dynamics to generate counterfactual training data. The
factored AC-LSCM is close to these models; our contribution is not the
architecture but a controlled measurement of which of its ingredients make a
consequence-aware planner safer, under protocols (the do-wrapper,
counterfactual data for every model, unseen multi-variable actions) that
separate them.

\paragraph{Causal representation learning.}
Sch\"olkopf et al.~\citep{scholkopf2021causal} survey the program of learning
representations aligned with causal mechanisms. CITRIS~\citep{lippe2022citris}
identifies causal factors from temporal sequences with known intervention
targets, and mechanism sparsity~\citep{lachapelle2022disentanglement} and weak
supervision from paired interventions~\citep{brehmer2022weakly} provide related
guarantees. Our latent AC-LSCM is a cautionary data point for this program in
a planning context: without such guarantees, a latent space trained for
prediction does not align with the action targets, and intervention
semantics applied to it hurt.

\paragraph{Continuous structure learning.}
NOTEARS~\citep{zheng2018notears} reformulated DAG learning as continuous
optimization with a smooth acyclicity function, and DAGMA~\citep{bello2022dagma}
improved its numerical behaviour. For lagged dynamics these constraints are
unnecessary, and in the first version they contributed to removing every edge.

\paragraph{Safe action selection.}
Shielding~\citep{alshiekh2018shielding} blocks actions whose consequences
would violate a formal safety specification, using a model of the
environment. Algorithm~\ref{alg:planning} is a learned, approximate analogue
with deferral when no action passes.

\paragraph{World models and risk for LLM agents.}
RAP~\citep{hao2023rap} uses an LLM as its own world model for planning,
WebDreamer~\citep{gu2024webdreamer} uses an LLM to simulate the outcome of
candidate web actions, and ToolEmu~\citep{ruan2024toolemu} uses an LM-emulated
sandbox to identify risky agent actions. CLadder~\citep{jin2023cladder} and
CounterBench~\citep{chen2025counterbench} document weaknesses of current LLMs
on formal causal queries. Our question, what structure a forward model needs
to get the consequences of interventions right, is complementary; we study it
where ground truth is available and have not connected it to language
models.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Synthetic, matched setting.}
The environments are low-dimensional SCMs whose mechanisms are lagged and
additive-noise, the same model class as AC-LSCM, with observations that are a
noisy copy of the state and actions with known targets. This is the setting
most favourable to the factored model. Whether its advantages survive
misspecified or instantaneous mechanisms, partial observability, unknown
intervention targets, or high-dimensional observations is untested; in the
last case the factors would have to be learned, and Section~\ref{sec:latent}
shows how that can fail.

\paragraph{No language-domain evaluation.}
The motivation concerns language-model agents; the evidence concerns forward
models in synthetic environments. Connecting the two, for example by
extracting variables and candidate actions from an agent's tool calls, is the
most important open step.

\paragraph{Scale of the study.}
Seven environments and three seeds per model give paired comparisons with
clear signs for rollout error and for two-variable actions, but the
single-variable comparison with the best baseline on ER-10 is within noise,
and the ER-20 results rest on two graphs. Baselines keep the first version's
hyperparameters and were not tuned for this study; neither was AC-LSCM beyond
the changes described in Section~\ref{sec:arch}. The task rule, the horizon,
and the candidate construction are one choice among many.

\paragraph{Planner.}
The planner uses point predictions and no uncertainty, and every model stays
far from the oracle. A planner with calibrated uncertainty might change the
ranking of models.

\section{Conclusion}

An agent that rejects unsafe actions by simulating them is only as good as
its forward model's grasp of indirect consequences. In controlled synthetic
environments, a forward model with one mechanism per observed variable, a
learned sparse graph, and correct intervention semantics predicts multi-step
consequences more accurately than unstructured world models in every paired
comparison, and gives the safest planner, with the largest margins where
structure should matter most: larger systems and combinations of actions not
seen in training. The same structure placed on learned latent variables does
worse than no structure. Correct action semantics, which the do-wrapper
provides to any model, account for much of the baselines' deficit; the
explicit do-operator, given the right variables, adds little. For agent
designers, the practical reading is to give the forward model the variables
that actions and safety conditions refer to, to impose known action
semantics rather than learn them, and to test it on consequences of actions
unlike those in its training data. This version also corrects the first one,
whose headline result did not hold.

\section{Experimental Details}
\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Models.}
All latent models use a two-layer MLP encoder and decoder (hidden size 128)
with a $K$-dimensional latent. AC-LSCM uses one transition MLP per factor
(input $K{+}1$: the masked parents and the action value, hidden size 32) and a
Gumbel-sigmoid adjacency with temperature $0.5$. The factored variant uses
the same transition with the identity in place of encoder and decoder.
Vanilla VAE uses a single MLP transition (hidden 128) on the concatenation of
latent and action; Dreamer-style uses a GRU cell with hidden size $K$. The
Transformer embeds each of the $2K$ scalars of $(\x_t, a_t)$ as a token
($d = 128$, 2 pre-norm layers, 4 heads, feed-forward 512), mean-pools, and
predicts $\x_{t+1}$ with an MLP head.

\paragraph{Optimization.}
Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 64, gradient clipping at 1.0,
up to 200 epochs with early stopping (patience 20) on a validation split
taken from the end of the training trajectory. The counterfactual loss, when
used, has weight 1. Exact abduction: the recovered noise is not added to
factors intervened on in either the factual or the counterfactual action.

\paragraph{One-step error.}
One-step intervention MSE compares the prediction from a noisy observation
with the next noisy observation. With observation and process noise of
standard deviation $0.1$ each, even a perfect model has an expected error
above $0.02$, and on ER-10 all models lie between $0.030$ and $0.046$
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), so differences in this metric are small. This is why we evaluate forward models by rollout error
against the noise-free true trajectory instead.

\paragraph{Compute.}
Runs used one CPU thread each: a few minutes for the factored model, 10 to
30 minutes for the other models, and up to three hours for the Transformer
on ER-20.
